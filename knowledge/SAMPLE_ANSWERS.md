# Pilot sample answers

## What does this creator recommend about backsweetening?

The collection shows several CSB approaches, not one universal rule. They taste before post-fermentation adjustments ([Creamsicle, 21:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tVJ07b6HdA&t=1270s)); in the allulose experiment they kept sweetness with a non-fermentable sweetener and reserved 42 g sugar for carbonation ([Allulose, 11:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TM62rfUZ6Xw&t=715s), [15:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TM62rfUZ6Xw&t=900s)); and in the apple-butter batch they backsweetened and then pasteurized the liquid center at 160 F for one minute ([Apple Butter, 41:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MoBq0QayjPY&t=2500s)). In the stabilization comparison, they describe chemical stabilization and pasteurization as tools and explicitly say one simple-mead comparison is not definitive ([Stabilizers, 32:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qupXap5BBOE&t=1950s)).

## What changed between a recipe video and its later tasting?

The Creamsicle recipe fermented from 1.088 to 0.992 before adjustment, which CSB calculated as 12.96% ABV ([recipe, 23:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tVJ07b6HdA&t=1380s)). It was later backsweetened to 1.028. At the one-year tasting they called that dessert-sweet ([tasting, 0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GvM3nQdeDu0&t=0s)) and reported that it had become smooth and mellow while retaining orange, vanilla, and honey character ([2:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GvM3nQdeDu0&t=135s), [3:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GvM3nQdeDu0&t=195s)).

## Which videos discuss a particular fermentation problem?

For sluggish fermentation, *Fermentation Fundamentals* advises waiting before adding more yeast in the discussed step-fed batch and suggests moving oak to conditioning if needed ([18:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=euOXXbi0f9A&t=1120s), [19:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=euOXXbi0f9A&t=1180s)). For an apparently inactive airlock, *What Homebrew Fermentation Should Look Like* says a poor seal can hide activity and recommends looking for bubbles in the liquid ([6:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv044DK1Ifc&t=390s)). It also covers clogged airlocks and vigorous foam ([4:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv044DK1Ifc&t=250s)). These are the creator's troubleshooting suggestions, not established rules for every fermentation.
